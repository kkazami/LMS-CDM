### Preserve decisions and functionality

- Institute administrators retain database tools, with the remediated institute/model/field limits; generic restore is existing-row editing, not snapshot recovery or arbitrary provisioning.
- Students send activity answers; the server computes scores and `passed`, normalized to coursework maximum. Course-owned submissions/test fetches must resolve a course and pass `requireCourseAccess`; no intended cross-course template sharing has been established.
- Completed form scores are visible to their student and authorized instructor across serializers. Do not reinstate the superseded hidden-score rule; answer-key and feedback controls are distinct.
- LMS authenticator apps and recovery codes are the selected privileged MFA approach. Real administrators enroll their own devices. Extending assurance to students/faculty, changing recovery UX or changing session rules needs owner consultation.
- Preserve the currently committed production provider policy: OnlineCompiler or authenticated reviewed Judge0; no production JDoodle or host-execution fallback. Changing provider trust, runtime architecture or supported assessed sketch/circuit behavior needs owner consultation.
- Obtain approval before changing business rules, tenant architecture/RLS, data retention, institutional privacy policy, historical grades, backup objectives, public sharing, native distribution or release architecture. Planning a decision does not authorize its implementation.

## Classification and verification rules

Implementation classification is separate from verification:

- **implemented**: the narrowly stated control has implementation plus retained behavioral evidence for its recorded scope. This does not imply every sibling, current release or deployment is verified.
- **partially implemented**: a control exists but known coverage, policy, deployment or operational pieces remain incomplete. Source presence alone fits here, not an effectiveness pass.
- **missing**: an explicit placeholder or missing artifact/control was identified in the inspected scope. This does not assume absence of uninspected hosting-side features.
- **unverified**: decisive evidence or applicability is unavailable, or validation is blocked. Do not convert a candidate into a confirmed finding.

Evidence markers: **H** = retained historical execution, **S** = limited source/configuration inventory, **D** = documentation, **U** = not verified, **B** = blocked. Tests listed are existing inventory; if only adjacent or mocked, the row says so. Fresh Sprint 1 checks are identified below and in its dated verification record; other test results retain their historical scope. `implemented` is never an ASVS pass by itself.

Priorities are **planning priorities**, not new vulnerability severities. **P0**: release decision/containment evidence needed because failure could affect academic records, private data, privileged access, infrastructure or recovery; unknown high-impact controls can be P0 without a confirmed defect. **P1**: next hardening milestone before claiming L2 readiness. **P2**: scheduled robustness and applicability work; raise priority if its concrete exposure warrants it. Preserve original finding severity/verdicts unchanged.

## Maintaining this central reference

1. Keep roadmap IDs stable. For every proposed change, link its PR/commit, affected ASVS IDs, business/architecture decision if any, regression tests and release dependency here. Add a new row for a genuinely different requirement; preserve old evidence.
2. Update **implementation classification** and **verification status** independently. A requirement needs an exact test/review result, scoped principal/resource, revision, date, environment, expected/observed outcome and reviewer before it can pass. For production-dependent controls, require current operator evidence as well.
3. In the 253-row register, `implementation_context`, source and tests are **related control-family context**, not proof for that individual requirement. Each `classification=unverified` stays open until a requirement-specific assessment. Known policy gaps are labeled partial. `applicability` is initially pending; non-applicability needs a signed feature/protocol/data-flow rationale and is separate from implementation status. 4. Preserve run1/run2 and regression/remediation history. Append dated superseding results rather than overwriting old verdicts/counts. Never rerun covered units merely to fill this roadmap. 5. Review the roadmap before every release and after significant auth, grading, tenant, storage, provider, API, native, dependency or CI changes. Reconcile operator facts and open incident flags. Periodic dependency/operational reviews are proposed process; no background automation is created here.
6. Keep credentials, student answers, recovery values and personal data out of the roadmap/register/test receipts. Use credential-free deployment identifiers and counts. Maintain an access-controlled evidence archive with checksums; do not commit private scratch output or dotenv files.
